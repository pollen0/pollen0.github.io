# Why the benchmark

Vishal Gupta (Executive Director at Mizuho) [put it this way](https://johnlothiannews.com/mizuhos-gupta-says-the-benchmark-not-the-contract-is-the-real-innovation-in-compute-futures/): "A compute future is not the innovation here; I think the benchmark is the real innovation."

His argument: compute is the new oil only in the loosest sense. It behaves more like electricity — oil can be stored and has a specific refinement value, while compute prices vary per region and depend on transmission and transmission losses (the movement of electricity from the site to the user).

The two sides of the market are clear. Neoclouds and hyperscalers want to sell compute and hedge against price swings, while AI model developers and large financial institutions want to buy compute and keep spending within budget. Between them sit different market prices from all the index providers — [Ornn](ornn.md) and [Silicon Data](silicon-data.md) among them.

Which raises the real question: how can you trust any of these benchmarks?

The standard compute unit is the GPU-hour — but a GPU-hour from a neocloud and a GPU-hour from a hyperscaler might not perform the same. Performance can depend on the chip, the region, and latency.

That points to an increasingly fragmented market, where each competitor only trades on platforms where they trust the benchmark — until the market naturally converges on one.
