# Why this market exists

As AI workflows get more ingrained in daily life — personal agents like Muse, Grok, Instinct, and Dots, and frontier labs pushing the bounds of frontier intelligence — compute keeps rising as the most important commodity.

(Much of the thinking below follows Aishwarya's Substack and Liquid Compute's published research.)

The analogy that keeps coming to mind is a farmer. Producers of compute are subject to the forces of demand in an opaque market, which can lead to large swings in profit. If there were a contract to serve as a hedging instrument — similar to a call option — they could reliably hedge against downward moves in compute prices.

Consumers of compute, whether frontier AI labs, new startups, or anything in between, benefit too: by buying contracts, they lock in a fixed price and don't have to deal with the uncertainty of future prices.

As I understand it, the main chokepoints in how the market has developed so far:

- **Contract timeline mismatch.** Buyers are incentivized to keep contracts short-term — precarious given how fast the compute, model, and GPU landscape changes.
- **Implied credit ratings.** Neoclouds whose main client base is clients with weak revenue status.
- **Non-uniform contracts.** As the name implies, contract terms aren't standardized.
