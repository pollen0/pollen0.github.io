From what I seen, the dominant players out there seem to be Ornn, Compute Desk, Liquid Compute, SF Compute, Silicon Data, Compute Exchange, Andromeda all addressing different parts of the market.

Lets breakdown each one as of today.

Ornn has two branches, Ornn Compute and Ornn Data. As part of Ornn Compute you can obtain access to all the compute that Ornn has negotiated with, lock in dedicated contracts and trade idle reservations in the secondary market.
This allows buyers and sellers to all aggregate in one place, putting Ornn's as the central marketplace for buyers and sellers to meet. With Ornn data you obtain all the gathered insights from the market such as the current buyers,
the average price of different GPUs named the OCPI (Ornn Compute Price Index). Ornn licenses its index to others such as partnering iwth ICE with Ornn on cash-settled futures, Architect's AX exchange planned perpetual futures on GPU and DRAM
prices using Ornn's data and Kalshi contracts on GPU prices resolve against Ornn's data. Ornn's current main value proposition lies in being able to be the de-facto reference price for all transactions and scale from there. They have raised $33 million led by A16z

Why the benchmark?
- VIshal Gupta (Executive Director at Mizuho https://johnlothiannews.com/mizuhos-gupta-says-the-benchmark-not-the-contract-is-the-real-innovation-in-compute-futures/) says that "A compute future is not the innovation here; I think the benchmark is the real innovation".
- He argues that Compute is the new oil, where it can be more linked to electricity, oil can be stored and has a specific refinement value where compute behaves more like electricity prices vary per region and depend on transmission and transmission losses (i.e the movement of electricty from the site to the user)
Naturally, neoclouds and hyperscalers want to sell compute and want to hedge against price swings, while AI model developers and large financial instituiosn want to buy compute and want to keep spending within budget. In betwen them sit different market prices from all the index providers listed above.
How can you trust each of the benchmarks above?

The normal compute unit is a GPU-hour but a GPU hour from a neocloud and hyperscaler might not perform the same, performance may depend on the chip, region and latency. 

This will lead to an increasingly fragmented market where each of the competitors above only trade on platforms where they trust the benchmark until the market naturally converges to one benchmark.

Why does this market even exist? 

The most common analogous this always comes to is a farmer which can be represented as producers of compute, they are subject to the forces of demand of compute
in an opaque market which can lead to large swings in profits, if there was a contract which can serve as a hedging instrument similar to a call option,
they can invariability hedge the downward forces of compute prices. 

In contrast, those who are consumers of compute whether they are the frontier AI labs, new start-ups or anything in between, they can also benefit with the rise 
of contracts in the compute market through buying contracts, where they are locked in to a fixed price and don't need to deal with the uncertainty of future price.

As I understand it now, the main chokepoint in how the market has currently been developed is
- Contract timeline mismatch: Buyers are incentivized to maintain short term contracts precaurious to future changes in the compute/mdodel/GPU landscape
- Implied credit ratings: Neoclouds whose main client base are clients with weak revenue status
- Non uniform contracts: As implied by the name
